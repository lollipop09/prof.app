PRD
Who is the customer?
* Demographic information: Broad appeal — ages 16–55, all genders, mixed socio-economic status. Core early adopters are likely 18–40: students, remote workers, and small teams who need messaging plus meetings in one place.
* Language: English at launch, with the UI built for localization from day one (string files, not hardcoded text) so you can add languages later without rework.
* Geographic region: Global by nature, but pick a launch region (e.g., North America) so you can handle data residency, compliance, and server locations deliberately.
* Access to internet: Assumes reliable broadband/4G for calls, but messaging should tolerate weak connections (offline queuing, auto-retry, low-bandwidth mode).
* Platform (OS): Windows, macOS, Android, iOS — with a web client as the lowest-friction entry point. This is your "compatibility with different OS" requirement.
* Hardware access: Requires microphone and camera permissions; must run acceptably on mid-range phones and older laptops, not just flagship devices.
Why → Target audience (Who)
Who: Students, remote workers, and small teams (roughly 18–40) who live across multiple devices and operating systems, and who currently juggle separate apps for chatting, calling, and meetings.
Why we exist: Communication today is fragmented. People message on one app, video call on another, and take meeting notes in a third. We believe staying connected and productive shouldn't require app-switching.
What → The user's problem we're solving
* Fragmentation: WhatsApp for chat, Zoom for meetings, Google Docs for notes — context and files get scattered across tools.
* Lost information: Decisions made in calls evaporate because nobody took notes, or notes live somewhere separate from the conversation.
* Platform lock-in: Some tools work great on one OS and poorly (or not at all) on others, forcing groups to settle on the lowest common denominator.
* Privacy anxiety: Users don't trust that their conversations and files are secure, and privacy settings are often buried or confusing.
Problem statement in one line: People waste time and lose information switching between disconnected communication tools that don't work equally well on every device.
How → Our approach
* One app for the full loop: message → share files → call → screen share → get an auto-generated summary, all in the same thread. The conversation and its outcomes live in one place.
* Cross-platform parity: identical core experience on Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, and web — 
* Security by default: encryption, 2FA, and clear privacy controls built in from day one, not bolted on.
* The differentiator: the meeting notes taker/summarizer turns ephemeral calls into searchable, persistent knowledge — that's what makes this more than "another WhatsApp clone."


Feature list — MuSCoW
Must have (Mu) — the MVP that communicates value:
* User interface
* Authentication protocol + 2-step verification
* Encryption (at minimum in transit and at rest)
* Database
* Adding contacts
* Messaging over internet (1-on-1)
* Sharing pictures/videos and documents
* Instant voice calls
* Video calls (1-on-1)
* Privacy controls (basic: who can contact you, block users)
Should have (S) — phase 2:
* Group chats
* Group voice calls
* Group video calls
* Screen sharing
* End-to-end encryption (if MVP shipped with transport encryption only)
Could have (Co) — good to have:
* Annotation tools
* Meeting notes taker/summarizer (high complexity — depends on transcription + AI services, so it earns its place only after calls are rock-solid)
* Advanced privacy controls (disappearing messages, read receipt toggles)
Wouldn't have (W) — feature bloat for this customer:
* Social feed/stories
* Payments/money transfer
* Games, stickers marketplace
* Public channels/broadcasting
The logic: 1-on-1 messaging and calling proves the core value. Group features are the natural phase 2 because they reuse the same infrastructure at higher scale. The AI summarizer is your differentiator but the riskiest build, so it waits until the foundation works.
